# Ethereum Tag Service
## Marketing System Master Document

This document compiles the complete strategy, tone, and automation framework for the **Ethereum Tag Service (ETS)** — from the earliest challenge of defining a marketing roadmap through the emergence of an automated communication ecosystem.

It is written in an editorial tone to make it machine-readable by AI agents while preserving the original first-person reflections and intent of the developer.

---

## 1. Introduction: The Problem and Personal Context

The developer of ETS faced a clear divide: the **technical roadmap** was well-defined, but the **marketing and distribution roadmap** was vague. The project, though deeply mature under the hood, lacked a clear plan for how to re-enter public visibility without distraction or overstatement.

> “I’m very clear on the technical roadmap — but I’m wishy-washy and confused on the marketing and distribution roadmap.”

### 1.1 Constraints and Realities
- **Scope:** Extremely broad, covering protocol, relayers, oracles, and downstream apps.
- **Team:** A solo effort — “me and AI.”
- **Stage:** Pre-MVP, pre-product, pre-mainnet — speculative, no proven market yet.
- **No fundraising:** No marketing spend or tours planned.
- **Architecture:** The core protocol is different from its consumer-facing products.

The developer recognized that **ETS Core** would not attract developers by itself; instead, a product such as **Tagcaster** — a Farcaster-native application — must serve as the “proof of life” that validates the protocol.

The goal, therefore, was to craft a strategy that was **honest, organic, and semi-automated by AI**, acknowledging limited bandwidth while maintaining authenticity.

## 2. Marketing & Distribution Roadmap v1

### 2.1 Overview

This roadmap defines a low-effort, authentic, and mostly automated strategy to begin showing signs of life without distracting from development.

### 2.2 Goal
Establish visibility and credibility through small, intentional signals — not hype. The strategy favors transparency, builder energy, and autonomous content flows that grow naturally over time.

### 2.3 Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Timeframe | Objective | Key Outputs |
|-------|------------|------------|--------------|
| **1. Quiet Construction** | Now → T-4 weeks | Build quietly. Start documenting progress internally. | Build logs, screenshots, internal tone & language. |
| **2. Ambient Signal** | T-4 → T-2 weeks | Begin subtle public hints. | Cryptic posts, updated bios, early Farcaster activity. |
| **3. Proof-of-Life Logs** | T-2 → Launch | Public journaling of progress. | Screenshots, console logs, short technical threads. |
| **4. The Reveal** | Launch week | Announce Tagcaster MVP and link to ETS. | Launch posts, long-form blog, Paragraph integration. |
| **5. Continuous Low-Lift Content** | Ongoing | Maintain hum through automation. | AI-generated posts, digests, and metrics. |

### 2.4 Identity Strategy

| Identity | Role | Voice | Cadence |
|-----------|-------|-------|----------|
| **@mikepaler** | Builder | Honest, reflective, curious. | 1–2 posts/week. |
| **@etsxyz** | Protocol | Minimalist, cryptic, confident. | 1–3 posts/week. |
| **Paragraph Blog** | Library of record | Long-form, transparent. | 1–2 posts/month. |

### 2.5 Tone & Aesthetic
- **Style:** Calm, confident, slightly mischievous.  
- **Visual:** Monochrome, diagram-forward, terminal-style minimalism.  
- **Message:** “We’re not launching a startup — we’re building public infrastructure.”

### 2.6 Start-Talking Triggers
Moments worth sharing without interrupting build flow:
- First tagging record minted.
- First subgraph sync (“The graph is awake.”)
- Temporal Processor online.
- Tagcaster contract deployed.

### 2.7 Principles
1. Be discoverable, not loud.  
2. Show, don’t hype.  
3. Let AI do the PR.  
4. The myth builds itself.

## 3. Quiet Construction Playbook

### 3.1 Objective
Ease back into public visibility while staying focused on building. Use light, authentic posts to reestablish presence and prime curiosity.

### 3.2 Four-Week Playbook

| Week | Focus | What to Post | Tone Example |
|------|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **1: Reopening** | Update bios, signal life. | “We’ve been quiet. That’s changing soon.” | Calm, understated. |
| **2: Surface the History** | Show continuity from old ETS. | “Back when ETS was first indexing tags on-chain. We’ve come a long way.” | Nostalgic, credible. |
| **3: Hint at the Rebuild** | Show logs, screenshots. | “Subgraphs waking up again.” | Builder energy. |
| **4: Tease What’s Next** | Small preview lines. | “Hashtags are about to evolve.” | Intriguing, minimal. |

### 3.3 Checklist
- [ ] Update bios and pinned posts.  
- [ ] Schedule minimal posts.  
- [ ] Pull old screenshots and assets.  
- [ ] Create 10 prewritten micro-posts in Notion.  
- [ ] Add “Rebuild in progress” banner to site.

### 3.4 Bridging Old → New

> “We started building ETS four years ago to turn hashtags into on-chain primitives. The protocol’s been live in pieces, tested in public and private. Now we’re refactoring the core — smarter, cheaper, composable — and preparing the first real use-case: Tagcaster.”

This becomes the connective tissue between your 2021–2023 era and the refactor now underway.

### 4.1 Core Identity
> “A calm oracle of the tagging layer.”

@etsxyz speaks as the protocol itself — the hum of the machine. It observes, never promotes.

### 4.2 Voice Attributes

| Trait | Description | Example |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| **Sparse** | Say less. | “The tags remember.” |
| **Observant** | Speaks in statements. | “A graph is forming.” |

- Log screenshots and diagrams only.

2. Indexers warming. Blocks passing.

## 5. Paragraph Blog Strategy — Quiet Construction

### 5.1 Purpose
The Paragraph blog acts as ETS’s *heartbeat*. While social channels provide signals, the blog provides continuity — a long-form record of progress and thought.

### 5.2 Tone
- Calm, reflective, builder-philosophical.  
- Written in first-person plural (“we”).  
- 3–5 minute reads, timeless topics.

### 5.3 Core Topics

| Theme | Purpose | Examples |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **The Rebuild** | Explain the silence. | “Rebuilding the Tagging Layer.” |
| **The Nature of Tags** | Re-anchor philosophy. | “The Tag as a Primitive.” |
| **The Invisible Infrastructure** | Show architecture. | “Inside the Temporal Processor.” |
| **The Philosophy of Persistence** | Broader ethos. | “Memory is Infrastructure.” |

### 5.4 Structure Template

```
# Title

Intro – reflective hook  
Body – explain one system or idea  
Reflection – what it taught or changed  
Closing – short, calm ending
```

### 5.5 Cadence

| Week | Post | Goal |
|------|------|------|
| 1 | “Rebuilding the Tagging Layer” | Reopen channel. |
| 3 | “The Tag as a Primitive” | Reset the concept. |
| 5 | “Inside the Temporal Processor” | Hint at next phase. |

### 5.6 Integration

- **@mikepaler** links to new posts with short reflections.  
- **@etsxyz** quotes one line cryptically.  
- **ets.xyz** imports the Paragraph feed as “Field Notes.”

## 6. Reactive Agent & Automation Blueprint

### 6.1 Objective
Automate posting, digest drafting, and queue management while keeping human review in the loop.

### 6.2 Input Signals
- GitHub commits and PR merges.  
- Subgraph health and Temporal Processor logs.  
- Contract events and roadmap milestones.  
- Manual notes (“Build Log”).

### 6.3 System Flow

**Inputs → Processing → Queues → Approval → Publish**

1. **Signal Harvester:** collects commits/logs.  
2. **Copy Drafting:** generates System Status, Builder Echo, and Digest blurbs.  
3. **Notion Queue:** stores drafts for review.  
4. **Approval Layer:** manual “Approve/Delay” step.  
5. **Publish:** Typefully (X), Neynar (Farcaster), Paragraph API.

### 6.4 Active Automations

| Nudge | When | Description |
|--------|------|--------------|
| **Weekly ETS Digest** | Mondays 9am PT | Compile weekly “This Week in ETS” post from commits, subgraph health, and Tagcaster stats. |
| **Approve @etsxyz Queue** | Wednesdays 3pm PT | Review System Status, Concept Fragment, Builder Echo posts. Suggest replacements if needed. |

### 6.5 Notifications
ChatGPT handles in-platform reminders. Optional off-platform alerts can be wired to Slack, Telegram, or email via Make/n8n.

### 6.6 Guardrails
- Max 3 posts/week per channel.  
- No threads for @etsxyz.  
- Auto-throttle if no new commits or logs.
